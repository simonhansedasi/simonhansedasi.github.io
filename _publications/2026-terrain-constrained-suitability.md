Climate projections routinely identify regions as agriculturally suitable based on thermal and precipitation envelopes alone. We demonstrate that terrain constraints systematically eliminate a substantial fraction of climatically suitable area, and that ignoring them produces overestimates of adaptation space that may misdirect investment and policy. The framework is demonstrated using specialty coffee in Hawaiʻi's Kona and Kaʻu districts at 500m resolution, incorporating terrain features derived from a 30m DEM alongside ERA5-Land climate variables and Sentinel-2 NDVI composites. Forward projections under NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 (SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5) extend to 2035 and 2045. The two districts diverge sharply under warming: Kona's thermal window contracts upslope, Kaʻu's expands.
